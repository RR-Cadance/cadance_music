Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers for teachers, musicians, and partners.

What is Cadance?

Cadance is a dance class music app built to keep classes moving with fast access, class‑friendly controls, and reliable playback. It's the sequel to BarreNotes.

Why doesn't Cadance support Spotify or other streaming services?

Cadance needs playback access that supports dance-specific controls such as tempo changes, repeats, countdowns, and reliable class playback. Spotify and most other streaming services do not provide third-party apps the playback rights and technical access needed for those controls. Cadance supports music in your device library, imported local files, Apple Music on supported tiers, and Syllabus material.

Coming from Spotify? Cadance includes a guide with two routes: copy your playlists to Apple Music with a transfer service such as TuneMyMusic or SongShift and then import them into My Cadance, or buy the tracks you teach with from the iTunes Store or Bandcamp so they play in Cadance with full tempo control. Find it in the Visual Tour under “How to Play Spotify Music” and “How Do I Import My Playlists?”.

What music licensing issues should studios and teachers consider?

Streaming services are intended for personal consumption. An Apple Music subscription and/or a Cadance subscription gives you access to playback tools; it does not itself grant public-performance or studio-use rights for the music.

Those rights are a publisher and rights-holder issue, separate from the tools used to play the music. Requirements vary by territory and use, so studios and teachers should contact the relevant publisher, rights holder, or local licensing body for guidance.

How can I try Cadance?

Download on the App Store: Cadance. Cadance is free to download and includes Class Planning Mode with browsing, search, playlist editing, Notebook, and 15-second previews. New users also receive 14 free Day Passes and 1 free Perfect Practice Pass.

How does Cadance pricing work?

Cadance combines free Class Planning Mode, flexible passes, and optional subscriptions. New users receive 14 free Day Passes and 1 free Perfect Practice Pass. A Day Pass unlocks a full teaching day; a Perfect Practice Pass keeps one selected song ready for 30 days. Day Passes never expire. See current local pricing in the App Store.

What can I do with Cadance for free?

Class Planning Mode includes browsing, playlists, Notebook, sharing, search and discovery, and 15-second previews of any track. Linked music can be read, shared, and sampled by everyone; full-length playback needs a pass or subscription that covers the track.

What happens when I play a track past 15 seconds?

The 15-second limit appears only when the track is not covered by your current subscription or other active access. For example, it applies when you have no subscription, or when you play an Apple Music or Syllabus track with a Spark subscription. If you have Day Passes, a banner shows “Activating Day Pass in 10s” while the music keeps playing. Let it activate to unlock the full teaching day. Once activated, you have full access until midnight local time — no further prompts, no interruptions during class.

If you choose “Don’t use a pass”, Cadance will not ask again during the current session, and tracks not covered by your current access stop at 15 seconds. To activate a pass manually, go to Settings › Account & Passes › Day Passes › Activate a Day Pass Now.

If you have no passes left, Cadance explains what unlocks the track: a subscription or a Day Pass, or for Syllabus tracks, Artist or a pass. Later stops in the same session show a short banner with a See Options button.

Do Day Passes expire?

No. Day Passes never expire. They stay in your account until you activate them, ready whenever you teach.

Do Day Passes sync between my iPhone and iPad?

Yes, via iCloud. Sign into the same iCloud account on both devices and your pass balance and purchase history are shared. An activated Day Pass runs on the device where it was activated and can’t be moved to another device. A Perfect Practice Pass track plays in full on all your devices.

What's in the curated Cadance Resources playlists?

70 playlists in 10 folders: Cadance Samplers from the Cadance music creator community, the Extraordinary Groups collection, Fresh Finds, Latin & Caribbean, Hip-Hop & R&B, Club & Electronic, Global Rhythms, Social & Era Dance, Contemporary & Recovery, and a selection of Apple Music playlists. Extraordinary Groups has purpose-built playlists for Parkinson's, Alzheimer's & Dementia, Autism Spectrum, Heart Health, MS & Stroke recovery, and Proactive Aging programs.

Cadance Resources playlists stream from Apple Music. Copy any playlist into My Cadance to edit your own version.

Which subscription tier is right for me?

Choose Day Passes for flexible full teaching days, or a Perfect Practice Pass when one song is the focus. Spark covers music stored on your device and imported files, with Apple Watch control; Artist adds Apple Music, Focus Player, and Syllabus playback. See current local pricing in the App Store.

How do I manage my subscription, restore purchases, or ask for a refund?

Open Settings › Account & Passes › Subscription. It shows your plan, renewal date, and price paid. Manage Subscription opens Apple’s subscription settings, Request a Refund opens Apple’s refund request for your current subscription, and Restore Purchases re-checks your purchases with the App Store. If a restore asks you to sign in, sign in to the App Store under Settings › your name › Media & Purchases and try again.

Day Pass and Perfect Practice Pass purchases are listed under Purchase History, which links to Apple’s Report a Problem page for refund requests.

Can I subscribe AND buy Day Passes?

Yes. A Day Pass temporarily unlocks Artist-tier access regardless of your current subscription. For example, a Spark subscriber can buy a Day Pass to access Apple Music for a one-off workshop without upgrading their subscription.

What if I run out of Day Passes mid-class?

That won't happen. Once a Day Pass activates, you have full access until midnight. Cadance will never interrupt a class. The only check happens at the 15-second mark of the first track of the day that needs a pass — well before any class begins.

What professional features does Cadance offer?

Cadance provides one Search for your music and Apple Music, Notebook, pro tempo control, repeats, Duck, a clock and class timer, Teaching Lock and Tilt Lock on iPhone, and a class-friendly interface designed for fast teaching workflows. Spark and above add full playback of music stored on your device and imported files, Delay Start, and Apple Watch control. Artist adds Apple Music, Focus Player, and Syllabus playback.

How does Search work?

Tap the magnifier at the top of the screen. Search looks through your device library, imported files, Syllabus, and playlists first, then shows Apple Music results below. Start by typing, or tap dance terms such as barre, allegro, pointe, or waltz; tap two or more to combine them. Use the All / Your Media switch to hide Apple Music results, or turn them off in Settings. Without an internet connection, Search shows your own media only.

What is Notebook?

Notebook is a free Class Planning Mode feature for typed and handwritten notes. Add Cadance music links to your notes, share them, and sample connected tracks in context. Notes sync between your iPhone and iPad through iCloud. Docking a note beside the music while you teach needs Artist, an active Day Pass, or an active Perfect Practice Pass.

What is Syllabus?

Syllabus is where your music libraries from syllabus programs, dance societies, and vendors live. Import their folders and zips and play the tracks in place, with text, PDFs, images, and video in a viewer beside the music. Playing Syllabus tracks needs Artist, a Day Pass, or a Perfect Practice Pass for the track; Spark doesn’t include Syllabus playback.

Who can use Focus Player?

Focus Player is available with Artist, while a Day Pass is active, and for a track on an active Perfect Practice Pass.

Do I need an Apple Music subscription?

No. Cadance works with music in your device library, imported local files, and Syllabus material. To play Apple Music tracks in full, you need your own Apple Music subscription plus Artist or a Day Pass. Without an Apple Music subscription, Apple Music tracks play Apple’s 30-second preview.

Does Cadance support tempo change and repeats?

Yes. Tempo control and repeats are core to Cadance. Change tempo from -50% to +100% without changing the pitch. Music you own changes tempo seamlessly; Apple Music tracks pause briefly at each change, an Apple limitation. Deeper guides are here: How To.

Can I import my own music?

Yes, this is a big improvement over BarreNotes! Tap + at the top of the screen and choose Import Music to pick folders and files from iCloud Drive or the Files app, or send them to Cadance with AirDrop or the Share sheet. Before a folder import, Cadance checks its size and your free space and warns before using mobile data. It shows progress, lets you pause, resume, or end the import, and resumes after you reopen Cadance.

Files already in Cadance are skipped, so importing again never creates duplicate copies. A Syllabus folder merges with your existing copy and asks before adding missing files, and a zip for the Syllabus is kept as it is until you tap to unpack it.

How do I learn Cadance?

Open the Visual Tour from the Favorites tab. It is built into the app, works offline, and starts with a five-tap Quick Start, followed by topics on passes, troubleshooting, Notebook, Apple Watch, and every control you use in class. Short feature videos are also on our How To page.

Is Cadance available on Android?

Not currently. Cadance is a native iOS/iPadOS app, optimized for Apple's audio, MusicKit, and Apple Watch integration. We're focused on delivering the best possible experience on Apple devices. Android support is not on the near-term roadmap.

What devices does Cadance support?

iPhone and iPad running iOS/iPadOS 16 or later, including both displays of iPhone Duo, and Macs with Apple silicon (see “Can I use Cadance on a Mac?”).

The Apple Watch companion app mirrors the track list open on your iPhone: play and pause, choose a track, change tempo with the Digital Crown or the + and − buttons, set the volume, duck, and rewind. Watch control covers everything your plan plays: Spark, Artist, or an active Day Pass.

A Bluetooth media remote, such as the Satechi R2, works with iPhone and iPad. On your own, Syllabus, and Local Storage tracks it plays and pauses, skips tracks, changes volume, and changes tempo in 1% steps when you hold Next or Previous. On Apple Music tracks it can play, pause, rewind, and change volume (an Apple limitation). On iPad, a large Remote Assist card shows the track and tempo across the room. Headphone buttons and presentation clickers aren’t supported.

Can I use Cadance on a Mac?

Yes, on a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later). Cadance is an iPad app that Apple makes available on these Macs as “Designed for iPad”. Open the Mac App Store, search for Cadance, and choose iPhone & iPad Apps in the results. Sign in with the same Apple Account as your iPhone or iPad to use your subscription on your Mac too; Notebook notes sync through iCloud. Macs with Intel processors can’t run it.

How do I get started?

Download Cadance from the App Store. You'll receive 14 free Day Passes and 1 free Perfect Practice Pass. On first launch, tap Continue and allow Media & Apple Music so Cadance can see your music; it then opens your albums or playlists, Cadance Resources, or Apple Music Search. Start the Visual Tour’s Quick Start from Favorites, then activate the access that fits the work you are about to do.

Definition of cadence

Cadence refers to a rhythmic sequence or flow of sounds in language, music, or activity. It can describe the beat or measure of rhythmical motion, such as in music or military chants.

Musical cadence

In music, a cadence marks the end of a phrase, creating a sense of resolution.

  • Authentic cadence: a strong resolution, typically moving from a dominant chord (V) to the tonic (I).
  • Half cadence: ends on a dominant chord, creating an incomplete feeling.
  • Plagal cadence: moves from the subdominant (IV) to the tonic (I), often called the "Amen cadence."
  • Deceptive cadence: leads you to expect a resolution that does not occur.
Cadence in language

In speech, cadence supports clarity and expression — the rise, fall, and timing that make phrases land.

Military cadence

In marching, cadence refers to rhythmic chants that help maintain pace and unity of movement.

Exercise cadence

Cadence can also refer to the rhythm or tempo at which a physical exercise is performed. In activities like running, cycling, or weightlifting, cadence is often measured in beats per minute (BPM), repetitions per minute (RPM), or steps per minute (SPM). Understanding cadence helps optimize performance, enhance endurance, and reduce injury risk.

Why cadence matters for dance classes

Cadence helps define structure and emotional impact in music, improves flow in language, and supports synchronization in movement. In class, that translates into clean starts, consistent tempo, and less interruption.
